# How Much Does a Website Cost in Nepal? (2026 Pricing Guide)

If you are searching "website cost in Nepal" or "how much does a website cost," you probably want a straight answer — not a vague "it depends." Here is the honest, numbers-based reality for 2026, whether you are a startup, an established business, or a non-profit in Kathmandu.

---

## The short answer

- **Personal portfolio / landing page:** NPR 15,000 – 60,000
- **Business website (5–10 pages):** NPR 40,000 – 150,000
- **E-commerce store:** NPR 150,000 – 500,000+
- **Custom web application / SaaS:** NPR 400,000 – 3,000,000+
- **Maintenance & support:** NPR 5,000 – 30,000 per month

These are working ranges, not quotes. The real price depends on what you are actually building, and this guide breaks down every factor so you can budget correctly.

---

## Why the price range is so wide

A "website" can mean anything from a single HTML page to a multi-vendor marketplace with real-time payments. The three biggest cost drivers are:

1. **Complexity** — brochure site vs. login system vs. real-time dashboards.
2. **Custom work** — a template-based site is cheap; custom logic is not.
3. **Integration** — payments (eSewa, Khalti, ConnectIPS), SMS, and third-party APIs add real development time.

### The Nepali market in 2026

The local market has matured. Most serious developers in Kathmandu now build on **Laravel (PHP)** and **React/Next.js**, with **Django** and **Node.js** as strong alternatives. Technology choice matters less than the developer's track record, but it does affect long-term maintenance and hiring.

---

## Website types and what you can expect to pay

### 1. Personal portfolio / landing page

A single page (or a few pages) that represents you or your small brand.

- **Typical budget:** NPR 15,000 – 60,000
- **What you get:** 1–5 pages, responsive design, contact form, basic SEO setup, hosting deployment.
- **Timeline:** 1–3 weeks.

### 2. Business website

The standard for hotels, clinics, restaurants, and consultancies in Kathmandu.

- **Typical budget:** NPR 40,000 – 150,000
- **What you get:** 5–10 pages, blog or news section, enquiry forms, WhatsApp integration, Google Maps, image galleries, basic on-page SEO.
- **Timeline:** 3–6 weeks.

### 3. E-commerce store

Where things start to get serious — products, carts, payments, and delivery.

- **Typical budget:** NPR 150,000 – 500,000+
- **What you get:** product catalog, cart and checkout, **Nepali payment gateway integration** (eSewa, Khalti, ConnectIPS, or FonePay), order management, admin dashboard, delivery tracking.
- **Timeline:** 6–12 weeks.

### 4. Custom web application / SaaS

The most expensive category — and the one with the highest return.

- **Typical budget:** NPR 400,000 – 3,000,000+
- **What you get:** multi-tenant systems, role-based access, automated invoicing, booking engines, real-time features, admin panels, API integrations.
- **Timeline:** 2–6 months.

---

## What silently increases the price

- **Payment gateways** — NRB licensing rules for payment providers, plus integration effort, add NPR 50,000 – 150,000 to a build.
- **Custom design** — a bespoke Figma design costs more than a template.
- **Real-time features** — WebSockets, live dashboards, and chat systems are complex.
- **Third-party integrations** — SMS, email marketing, HR, accounting (Tally), or accounting software sync.
- **Content migration** — moving old content, images, and SEO equity adds hours.
- **Speed and SEO** — Core Web Vitals optimization and schema markup take expertise most "cheap" developers skip.

---

## Cheap vs. right: what NPR 20,000 actually buys

A very cheap website is almost always a template with your logo on it. It may look fine in a screenshot and fail badly in the real world:

- **2–3 second load times** on mobile, hurting Google rankings.
- **No structured data**, so Google can't understand your business.
- **No payment security** planning for e-commerce.
- **No maintenance plan**, leaving you stranded when things break.

The "expensive" developer who builds fast, SEO-ready, maintainable code usually costs **less over three years** than the cheap one you keep paying to fix.

## How to get an accurate quote

When you contact developers in Kathmandu, provide this up front:

1. **Goal** — what must the site achieve? (sell products, book rooms, generate leads)
2. **Pages/features list** — every page and every function you can think of.
3. **Integrations** — which payment gateways, SMS, or tools you need.
4. **Timeline** — when do you need it live?
5. **Budget range** — be honest; it helps the developer scope realistically.

A developer who gives you a price without asking these questions is quoting a template, not your project.

---

## Payment plans & maintenance

Most Kathmandu developers work in **50/40/10 milestones** (50% to start, 40% at review, 10% at launch) or **40/30/30**. Some offer monthly retainers that include hosting, backups, updates, and small changes — worth it if you want the site to stay secure.
